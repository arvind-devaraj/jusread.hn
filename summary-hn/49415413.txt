The post titled "Attention is all we have: A conjectural theory of cognitive inequality" explores the idea that cognitive abilities and intelligence may be influenced more by the allocation of attention and early habits than by innate genetic factors. The author suggests that how individuals focus their attention can significantly impact their cognitive development and potential.

In the comments, there is a discussion about the nature versus nurture debate, with one commenter referencing László Polgár's experiments with his daughters, which support the notion that with the right environment and training, individuals can develop exceptional skills, reinforcing the idea that "geniuses are made, not born." Another commenter acknowledges the role of genetics, noting that while some individuals may have natural advantages in physical or intellectual domains, this does not negate the importance of attention and practice in achieving excellence.

Overall, the discussion highlights a balance between genetic predispositions and the influence of environment and effort in shaping cognitive abilities, with a general agreement on the significance of early habits and focused attention in fostering talent.
