Agents don't need memory, they need documentation  (223 pts, 126 comments)
https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49945933

The Hacker News discussion revolves around the idea that AI agents, particularly in programming contexts, do not require memory in the traditional sense but rather need well-structured documentation to function effectively. The original post argues that while human programmers can rely on their mental models, AI requires explicit information to understand and extend code.

In the comments, there is a debate about the effectiveness of different documentation strategies and how they relate to AI's ability to learn and adapt. Some users emphasize the importance of maintaining a clear and evolving specification that aligns with the code, while others question the necessity of complex memory systems when session history can provide sufficient context. There are also discussions about the challenges of keeping documentation relevant and the potential for agents to generate excessive or irrelevant information.

Notable insights include the idea of using guiding principles instead of memory, which can help shape the decision-making process of agents. Some participants share their experiences with various documentation methods and tools, highlighting the balance between providing enough context for AI agents while avoiding information overload. Overall, the conversation reflects a growing interest in optimizing the interaction between AI and programming through effective documentation rather than traditional memory solutions.
