Show HN: Training a model to identify AI web content from structure alone  (23 pts, 7 comments)
https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49800566

The Hacker News discussion revolves around a project by Vincent and Jochen from Sitefire, which aims to train a model to distinguish between AI-generated and human-written web content based solely on structural features. They found that AI-generated content, often referred to as "AI slop," tends to follow predictable patterns, such as repetitive messaging and a lack of depth, allowing their classifier to achieve 98% accuracy in identifying the source of blog posts. They also developed tools for users to check their own writing against these structural markers.

In the comments, there is a mix of skepticism and critique regarding the methodology and implications of the project. Some users express concerns about the reliance on AI models for establishing thresholds and the potential for the tool to merely elevate the quality of "slop" rather than eliminate it. Others highlight the irony of AI-generated comments being misclassified as human, questioning the effectiveness of the slop detector. A notable opinion suggests that the project could benefit from identifying poorly written human content from before the AI era to better understand the nuances of "slop."

Overall, the discussion reflects a broader debate about the quality and originality of AI-generated content, the challenges of distinguishing it from human writing, and the implications of using AI to improve content creation.
